# Law-firm branding process for Weblyfe clients

Use this reference when a lawyer/law-firm project moves from website refresh into full brand identity, logo, moodboard, and client-facing proposal work.

## Key lesson
For legal services, the brand is not just decoration. It must create trust, authority, confidentiality, and recognizability while staying legally credible. Do not jump from current site audit straight to a prettier page. Run a branding phase before final UI if the client wants a high-end repositioning.

## Discovery inputs
- Current website and existing logo/wordmark.
- Public lawyer/firm facts: NOvA, KVK, address, practice areas, team size.
- Client call transcript, sanitized into project-relevant notes only.
- Competitor patterns: local premium firms, niche high-conversion firms, and legal lead-gen sites.
- Real-world assets available: portraits, team photos, office, video, logo source files.

Keep family/private context out of project docs unless it changes the business goal. Convert raw call transcripts into business-relevant notes.

## Branding workflow
1. **Brand audit**
   - What exists now?
   - What feels generic, cliché, or inconsistent?
   - What should be preserved, e.g. trust cue, name clarity, existing blue palette?

2. **Brand strategy**
   - Positioning statement.
   - Desired visitor perception.
   - Brand personality.
   - Trust/compliance boundaries.
   - Naming hierarchy: public brand, legal/commercial name, possible shorthand.

3. **Moodboard directions**
   Produce 2-3 directions before locking the logo. For premium law firms, useful directions are:
   - executive counsel: suit, confidence, navy, controlled authority;
   - confidential room: warm office, desk, notebook, privacy;
   - local authority: city/business context, accessible local presence.

4. **Logo exploration**
   Explore systems, not isolated marks. Required routes:
   - premium wordmark;
   - monogram/lettermark for compact use;
   - subtle abstract legal mark, e.g. column/path/document cue;
   - restrained emblem only if it stays modern and scalable.

5. **Brand system**
   Define colors, typography, iconography, imagery, motion, UI usage, and application rules.

6. **Application preview**
   Show how the identity works in context: website header, favicon, business card, email footer, Google Business Profile, proposal/PDF.

## Logo package checklist
- Full horizontal logo.
- Stacked logo.
- Wordmark only.
- Mark only.
- Light-on-dark version.
- Dark-on-light version.
- One-color black and one-color white.
- Favicon sizes: 16, 32, 192, 512.
- Social avatar.
- Business card or letterhead mockup.

## Legal branding pitfalls
Avoid:
- gavels;
- scales of justice;
- fake courtrooms;
- stock handshakes;
- over-ornate crests;
- unsupported specialist/outcome claims;
- implying a larger legal team than exists;
- showing team roles in a way that implies legal authority incorrectly.

For small firms, a strong pattern is: premium personal authority + transparent team support + direct contact.

## Asset brief pattern
Ask for originals, not compressed chat images:
- hero portrait in suit;
- office/desk/dossier shots with no sensitive data;
- team portraits if approved;
- local context imagery;
- short horizontal iPhone video clips for subtle hero/banner motion.

Video does not need high production value. Calm, real 5-10 second clips can make the site feel owned instead of templated.

## SEO/content extension
If the client wants authority growth, propose a reviewed content engine, not uncontrolled AI publishing:
- AI researches topics and drafts articles;
- lawyer reviews legal correctness;
- publish only after approval;
- build around service + location intent without thin city spam;
- add disclaimers and calm CTAs.

## Operational website extension
For law firms, the website can also reduce admin friction:
- hidden/semi-public `/intake` page;
- multistep form with minimal facts first;
- email papertrail;
- confidentiality warnings;
- no AI processing of sensitive client facts unless a separate secure architecture is explicitly designed.
