# Law-firm branding and growth-system pattern

Use this reference when a Weblyfe legal/law-firm client project expands from a website refresh into a brand, content, intake, and local-presence system.

## Core lesson
For boutique law firms, the site is not just a prettier landing page. It can become:
- a premium trust surface;
- a personal-authority brand;
- a local SEO/content engine;
- a controlled intake/onboarding system;
- a Google Business Profile and review foundation.

Keep the legal/compliance restraint from the legal-services playbook, but do not make the brand bland. Premium legal clients often need a stronger personal visual presence than generic `confidential counsel` language alone.

## Brand process sequence
1. Audit the current site and existing logo/assets.
2. Extract the desired client personality from calls/transcripts.
3. Build a brand strategy before logo generation: positioning, audience, values, proof, tone, boundaries.
4. Create a moodboard before final logos. Include suits/portraiture, office context, local city cues, documents/books, and palette/typography references.
5. Explore logo systems, not isolated marks:
   - primary wordmark;
   - compact mark or monogram;
   - stacked lockup;
   - light/dark versions;
   - favicon/social avatar;
   - business card/email-footer use.
6. Present applications early so the logo is judged in context, not as a floating graphic.

## Premium legal visual direction
A strong direction for a modern Dutch lawyer can be described as:
> Harvey Specter energy, translated to Dutch legal credibility: sharp, premium, confidential, personal, and restrained.

This means:
- deep navy/ink base;
- warm ivory background;
- champagne/gold accents;
- tailored portrait photography;
- quiet office/document details;
- confident copy without outcome promises;
- no stock legal clichés.

Avoid:
- gavels;
- scales of justice;
- fake courtrooms;
- generic handshakes;
- aggressive law-billboard tone;
- unsupported `best lawyer` claims.

## Asset brief
Ask for original, uncompressed files. WhatsApp-compressed images are a last resort.

Must-have:
- strong portrait of the lawyer in suit;
- team portraits if team is real and approved;
- office/context photography;
- logo/source files if they exist.

Nice-to-have:
- horizontal iPhone video clips, 5-10 seconds each;
- walking/office/dossier shots;
- staged handshake or consultation shot only if safe and non-sensitive;
- desk, bookcase, pen, paper, meeting details.

Rules:
- no real client data visible in documents;
- no misleading team/team-size implication;
- no sensitive matter shown in video.

## Website as operations system
For law firms, consider optional hidden or semi-public utility pages:
- `/intake` for structured first contact;
- `/voorbereiding-eerste-gesprek`;
- document checklist pages by practice area;
- process/onboarding explanation pages.

Intake forms should:
- collect minimal basic info;
- route to email or CRM;
- create a paper trail;
- warn users not to submit full sensitive dossier info yet;
- state that no attorney-client relationship starts until conflict checks and written engagement.

Do not route sensitive intake content through AI unless a separate safe/local architecture is explicitly designed and approved.

## Blog/SEO engine for law firms
A blog/knowledge base can build authority and local search presence, but legal content needs human review.

Safe workflow:
1. Agent researches topics and search intent.
2. Agent drafts concepts/articles.
3. Lawyer reviews legal accuracy and nuance.
4. Publish only after approval.
5. Monitor Search Console/Analytics and update periodically.

Useful clusters:
- practice area + city combinations;
- common legal first-step questions;
- term/deadline explanations;
- document checklists;
- process explainers.

Avoid thin city spam. Each local page needs useful context and real local relevance.

## Google Business Profile
For local legal clients, treat GBP as a first-class deliverable:
- create or claim profile;
- verify with client;
- add logo, photos, services, opening hours, website;
- prepare review request flow;
- only request reviews in ethically appropriate situations approved by the lawyer.

## Domain strategy
Do not over-focus on premium domains early. Keep the current trusted domain unless there is a strong brand reason to change. Short/premium domains can be parked or considered later, with old domain redirecting to the new one if adopted.
